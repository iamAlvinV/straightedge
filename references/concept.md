# Concept

The part AI absorbs last. Idea, intent, tension, reduction, memorability.

## Write the objective before opening software

Hard rule. It names:

- Who it is for.
- The one emotion it should carry.
- The business goal.
- Why it needs to exist.

State it as a testable sentence. "Premium but accessible." "Attract younger buyers without alienating older ones." The objective is the tiebreaker. When two choices compete, keep the one that serves what was written down.

Frame the problem before touching color, font, or texture. If the problem cannot be stated, the design is not ready to start. The client is paying for an outcome: clarity, trust, attention, positioning. Not pixels.

## Define what success is

Two kinds of job. Name which one before designing.

- **Result.** Signup, click, read, recall, purchase. Design backward from the outcome. A design the client likes that underperforms has failed. One they find only okay that hits the goal has won.
- **Resonance.** Brand, editorial, cultural, expressive. Judged on meaning, craft, and resonance. Do not apply a conversion test to this.

Most work is one or the other. Getting it wrong is how a brand piece gets killed for not converting and a landing page gets shipped because it looked nice.

## Find the one idea

- One piece, one dominant idea. Decided before opening software.
- If it cannot be said in a single line, it is not ready.
- Iconic comes from a single unmistakable idea readable at a distance, not from technical polish. Polish is not the differentiator.
- Strong-but-flat work makes you an asset producer. Controlling attention on purpose makes you a creative director.

## Communication problem first

Begin with the problem, not the layout. What helps the viewer understand and want the thing. Add only what solves that. Every added element earns its place.

Building a layout and hoping it works is the failure mode this exists to stop.

## Shape the feeling

Every piece sets a feeling before it delivers information. Decide it on purpose.

- Match the feeling to the real audience and the real brand. Premium styling aimed at a low-budget audience misses. Cheap styling on a premium brand misses.
- Carry it with type choice, color, layout, and the choice between flat color and gradient. These are the levers, not afterthoughts.
- The hero and the feeling land through image, size, color, and layout, not through text. If the viewer has to read words to get the point, the first glance failed.

## Incongruity thresholds (hard)

The unexpected-element move works inside a band.

- **Moderate incongruity only.** A resolvable surprise, the odd element that still makes sense on second look.
- **No extreme incongruity.** Chaos performs worse than both a moderate surprise and a clean expected design. Inverted U, not a slope.
- **One disruption per piece.**
- **The surprise attaches to the brand or message.** The odd element gets remembered on its own. The brand does not come along for free.
- **Test.** Name the one thing a viewer remembers ten seconds later. If it is the gag and not the brand, the design has not passed.

## Reduction (hard)

- As little design as possible. Strip every element that does not carry the idea. Less, but better. Not less for its own sake, less so the essential reads.
- More layers, effects, or detail do not equal a better design. Complexity is not a quality signal.
- Push the concept, not the software. Boundary-pushing belongs in the idea, not the layer count.
- Run a dedicated reduction pass on every piece before export.
- Simplicity is subtracting the obvious and adding the meaningful. Do not strip meaning while cutting noise.
- Making unimportant things quieter is often faster than making important things bigger.
- Reduction has a floor. Do not cut past the point where the message survives. Pair it with organization. A stripped, disorganized piece is still a failure.

## The justify pass

After the piece feels finished, explain each decision out loud as if presenting. Why this size, this position, this color, for this element. Anything that cannot be justified is the weakest part. Fix it or cut it. This is design rationale and critique, a real practice, not a habit.

## Cut to the core

For an event or announcement the core is usually: what is it, what to look at, when, and one grouped footer for everything else. Group secondary information (sponsors, credits, logistics) into one tight proximity cluster, one muted line where possible.

## Intelligent imperfection (guide)

- Break symmetry on purpose. Asymmetric balance reads dynamic. Strict symmetry reads static.
- Imperfection must be intentional. Deliberate disruption is the skill. Sloppiness is not.
- Let elements overlap, intersect, or sit slightly off, then refine until the whole holds. Cohesion is the test, not neatness.
- Do not fake analog carelessly. Digitally faked realness reads cheaper than clean digital craft.
- Match to audience. Tech and creative can push wonky. Regulated sectors stay subtle.
- This is partly a current trend, not a timeless law. Do not force wonk onto work that needs authority.

## Memorability

- Technically fine is the baseline now. Competent is everywhere.
- The most memorable work is clearer, simpler, and more emotionally direct.
- Simple in execution, distinct in concept. Distinctiveness is judged against everything around it, not in isolation.
- Do not hide behind complexity to look impressive.

## AI in the workflow

- Use it for ideation, exploration, and combinations you would not reach alone.
- Do not use it for the final selection, the refinement pass, or the consistency guarantee across a system.
- Never ship raw output as final. It is a draft that enters the process, not a deliverable that leaves it.
- Human QA every generated asset: hallucinated details, changed proportions, style drift, spelling.
- Single-subject consistency is solved. Full brand-system coherence across packaging, social, web, motion, and type is not. That needs a fixed reference or a custom-trained model plus QA on every asset.
- AI visuals often look right at first glance and wrong on a longer look. Give the work the longer look.

## Building taste

- Taste is selection: what you remove, what you simplify, what direction you commit to. It is a trainable evaluative skill.
- Restraint beats volume. AI overproduces. The edge is cutting.
- Study outside design 15 to 20 minutes a day: architecture, film, fashion, photography, packaging, real signage.
- Do not run the whole inspiration diet on Behance, Dribbble, and Instagram. That is how work starts to look identical.
- Pattern collision is the engine. Combine ideas from different worlds.
- Use constraints as a tool: one typeface, black and white only, one shape language, no gradients. A healthy dose helps. Too many stifle. It is a curve.
- Keep a swipe file with a note on why each piece grabbed you.

## Testing

- **5-second test.** Show the piece to fresh eyes for about five seconds, hide it, ask what they remember and what it is about. Do not warn them about the limit. Run it on five or more people. If they list extras or get stuck, cut again and retest.
- **Squint test.** Blur it. The right element must dominate.
- **Recall check.** Ten seconds later, is it the brand or the gag.
